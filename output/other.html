United Nations experts said his detention is “arbitrary and illegal”, and press and rights organisations, including the Committee to Protect Journalists, Reporters Without Borders and Index on Censorship itself, called for his release on the second anniversary of his detention.

Madbouly to dentistry student: The state stopped hiring all graduates a long time ago

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly said on Thursday, October 29, 2026, that the state stopped “a long time ago” committing to hire every graduate, and that the assignment system that covered all graduates of medical faculties is no longer suitable, during his meeting with university students in Suez Governorate, on the sidelines of his opening of the first phase of the Suez University Hospital.

Madbouly’s remarks came in response to a fourth-year dentistry student who told him: “Right now, as the dentistry sector, there is no assignment, the numbers are very high,” and asked either for assignment under a certain system or for a solution that reduces the numbers. The prime minister replied: “In the past, the state at one point used to say whoever graduates, I will hire.. the state stopped this a long time ago,” and added: “We have a culture that we all want to secure a government job.”

Madbouly explained that the government is reorganizing admission numbers at medical faculties, as it has begun increasing admissions to faculties of human medicine, while reducing numbers in specialties including dentistry and pharmacy. He said there are cases in which between 20 and 25 dentists share a single chair, while the actual need does not exceed five or six dentists.

At the same meeting, Madbouly said in response to a nursing student that incomes in Egypt “are not at a level that can be compared with other countries,” pointing to the president’s directive at the Egypt Forum to gradually raise the minimum wage from 8,000 pounds ($165.63) to 15,000 pounds ($310.56) over two or three years, and to the fact that the government apparatus employs 4 million people.

Cairo Governor Ibrahim Saber decided on Thursday, October 29, 2026, to halt tree pruning work at the Children’s Garden in Heliopolis and to refer the case to the Public Prosecution, after an inspection committee confirmed “excessive pruning” of some trees in the garden, which is run by the Heliopolis Association.

Cairo Governorate said in a statement that the committee was formed in coordination with Minister of Local Development and Environment Manal Awad, from the General Authority for Cleanliness and Beautification of Cairo and Nozha District, after news reports and social media posts about the cutting of the garden’s trees circulated. The governorate attributed the referral to the association’s officials cutting the trees without consulting specialists or obtaining the necessary approvals, and without following proper technical pruning procedures.

The incident follows a wide wave of anger sparked by the cutting of trees on streets in Greater Cairo last September, after which the Cairo governor pledged that no more trees would be felled and that pruning would be limited to cases of absolute necessity, and the Ministry of Environment instructed local councils not to allow the removal of any tree without written permission from the governor.

Journalists Syndicate forms committee to appeal Mada Masr closure ruling, calls for December 3 conference

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The council of the Journalists Syndicate decided on Thursday, October 29, 2026, to form a legal committee to prepare an appeal against the ruling of the New Cairo Misdemeanor Court against journalist Lina Attalah, editor-in-chief of Mada Masr and a member of the syndicate, and to follow all levels of litigation, in addition to forming a committee to communicate with state institutions in preparation for a conference the syndicate will hold on December 3.

The council said in its statement that it respects the independence of the judiciary, that failing to respond to licensing applications and failing to notify applicants are procedural violations, and that the proper path is completing procedures and administrative appeal, not criminal accountability. It added that the absence of fair and transparent licensing rules opens the door to pursuing those running websites through means including blocking. The council also decided to call for a meeting between the syndicate and the websites that have applied for licenses from the Supreme Council for Media Regulation to discuss the obstacles to issuing them.

The council demanded licensing rules applied equally to all, obliging the Supreme Council for Media Regulation to issue a reasoned, written decision within a set period that can be appealed, treating a lack of response as approval of the license under existing provisions, reviewing the legal status of blocked websites operating in Egypt, ending blocking by administrative decision, and halting prosecutions related to the lack of a license while granting a period to regularize status. It also demanded the release of journalists imprisoned in cases related to journalistic work and freedom of publication, the issuance of a freedom of information law, and a ban on custodial penalties in publishing cases.

The New Cairo Misdemeanor Court had ruled on Tuesday, October 27, to close Mada Masr and confiscate its equipment, and fined Lina Attalah 3 million pounds ($62,112) on a charge of running a website without a license. Attalah was detained for hours before being released after paying the fine, and during the release procedures it emerged that an in-absentia ruling had been issued in 2024 sentencing her to one year in prison, in a case on charges of spreading false news and running a website without a license, and her defense filed an objection to the ruling, to be heard on November 11.
